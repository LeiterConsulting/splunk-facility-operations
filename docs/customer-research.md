# Customer needs and demonstration scenarios

This appendix records the public sector discovery that informed the first proof of concept. It translates public priorities into generic operations scenarios for presenters and product development; it is not a claim about a customer's current systems. Agency names are excluded from product labels and shipped demo evidence. Sources were reviewed on October 4, 2026; dated findings and plans remain dated evidence.

Use the [operations value guide](operations-value.md) for the cross-sector story and evaluation measures, and the [presentation guide](demo-guide.md) for the actual walkthrough. Commercial business functions and industry-specific interface stories are the next app stage. Initial agency discovery does not restrict the underlying operations questions to government organizations.

## Initial priorities

| Research context | Public evidence | Proposed demonstration and discovery question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| CMS | The FY2026 Congressional Justification describes AI and robotic process automation opportunities in administrative work, including benefits-related processes. It is a budget document, not proof of deployed autonomous operations. [CMS justification](https://www.cms.gov/files/document/fy2026-cms-congressional-justification-estimates-appropriations-committees.pdf) | **Public Services and Benefits:** shared identity disruption delays eligibility and benefits access. Trace the dependency, show queued work and ownership, investigate chronology, and review a supervised recovery. Ask which functions share identity, integration, network, and external partner dependencies. |
| HHS headquarters | The December 2025 AI Strategy discusses internal operations, shared infrastructure, workforce support, and governance. It provides a basis for demonstrating governed investigation and automation; it does not establish an autonomous remediation deployment. [HHS AI Strategy](https://www.hhs.gov/sites/default/files/hhs-artificial-intelligence-strategy.pdf) | **Enterprise Shared Services:** show one shared-service incident affecting several business functions, a common briefing for executives, and different evidence views for engineering and security. Ask where dependency ownership and cross-team incident context are maintained. |
| HHS assurance context | The FY2025 FISMA review, published March 24, 2026, assessed controls as of July 31, 2025. Its recommendations include asset and software inventory, continuous monitoring information, privileged access approvals, and business impact analysis. Findings are audit-period evidence, not a statement of October 2026 remediation status. [HHS OIG review](https://oig.hhs.gov/reports/all/2026/review-of-the-department-of-health-and-human-services-compliance-with-the-federal-information-security-modernization-act-of-2014-for-fiscal-year-2025/) | **ISSO / CISO / Audit:** display missing evidence, age, ownership, illustrative control associations, and a decision record. Ask how expected assets are reconciled with telemetry and how an investigator identifies stale evidence. The demo does not calculate compliance or an authorization decision. |
| IHS | The Health Information Technology Modernization Program describes PATH EHR modernization and enterprise support, with interoperability and continuity among its priorities. [IHS modernization](https://www.ihs.gov/hit/) | **Distributed Healthcare:** correlate remote-site connectivity, shared systems, and clinical workflow impact. Show physical continuity and modernization change gates. Ask how remote sites distinguish local outages, enterprise failures, and incomplete observations. |

Initial stakeholder discovery highlighted enterprise dependency visibility, situational awareness gaps and interest in autonomy. Treat these as hypotheses to validate with each customer. The public sources above support related priorities; they do not independently establish those exact gaps in a named customer environment.

## How to make each presentation useful

Start with a business function the audience owns. Show how a shared or physical dependency changes its operating state, which evidence supports that conclusion, and who can investigate it. Switch the audience to demonstrate the same incident as an engineering investigation, a security evidence review, a CCB decision, and an executive consequence briefing.

Ask the assistant to separate observations from hypotheses, follow dependencies, inspect the event sequence, and draft a report with evidence. Then use supervised simulation to show an approval, recovery, and fresh verification. Use the missing physical-access observation to demonstrate that incomplete evidence remains unknown and blocks actions when it falls inside the action scope.

Before using customer data, agree the business functions, dependency authority, inventory scope, sampling intervals, access roles, approved model destination and evaluation measures. These decisions turn the generic demonstration into a relevant customer proof of concept. Any production action integration requires separate development and qualification.
